Part 4: Optimization & Reward Math

Differential Geometry (Theory)

Formulating the geometry of the road.

To incentivize the policy $\pi$ to navigate a complex track, we must mathematically encode the geometry of the track into the Markov Decision Process reward function $R$. One critical feature is providing the agent with the curvature of the upcoming path.

Discrete Calculus for Curvature ($\kappa$)

In differential geometry, the curvature $\kappa$ of a planar curve is defined as the rate of change of the tangent vector's angle with respect to arc length: $\kappa = \frac{d\theta}{ds}$.

Because our track is parameterized by discrete waypoints $w_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$, we approximate this continuous derivative using forward finite differences.

TRACK_MANAGER.PY (Pre-computation) 
# wp_np: [N, 3] -> [X, Y, Yaw]
for i in range(n_wps):
    i1 = (i + 1) % n_wps
    
    # Discrete dTheta
    dyaw = wp_np[i1, 2] - wp_np[i, 2]
    while dyaw > np.pi: dyaw -= 2 * np.pi
    while dyaw < -np.pi: dyaw += 2 * np.pi
    
    # Discrete ds (Euclidean distance)
    ds = np.linalg.norm(wp_np[i1, :2] - wp_np[i, :2])
    
    if ds > 0:
        kappa[i] = dyaw / ds
MATHEMATICS

We iterate over every discretized waypoint on the manifold.

Compute $\Delta \theta$ (the change in yaw). The while loops ensure the angle difference is mapped to the principal branch $[-\pi, \pi]$ to avoid artificial $2\pi$ discontinuities at the boundaries.

Compute $\Delta s$, the $L_2$ norm (Euclidean distance) between the points.

Approximate the derivative: $\kappa \approx \frac{\Delta \theta}{\Delta s}$. This scalar $\kappa$ is injected directly into the agent's state vector $S$.
