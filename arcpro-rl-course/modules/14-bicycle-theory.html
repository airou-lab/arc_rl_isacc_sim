Part 5: Action Kinematics

Kinematic Bicycle Model (Theory)

Translating policy output into rigid body joint actuations.

The policy $\pi_\theta$ outputs a bounded continuous scalar $a \in [-1, 1]$ representing a desired steering action. However, autonomous vehicles possess two separate steering joints (front-left and front-right). If both wheels turn at identical angles during a curve, the wheels will slip due to mismatched turning radii. This violates the non-holonomic constraints of the vehicle.

Ackermann Steering Geometry

To satisfy the no-slip condition, we map the policy's single scalar output into two distinct joint angles using Ackermann steering geometry, which is a variation of the Kinematic Bicycle Model.

MATHEMATICS 
# The Bicycle Model
R = L / tan(δ)

# The Ackermann Expansion
δ_inner = arctan(L / (R - w/2))
δ_outer = arctan(L / (R + w/2))
EXPLANATION

By approximating the car as a bicycle, the turning radius $R$ is defined by the wheelbase $L$ and the virtual center steering angle $\delta$.

We expand the bicycle back into a four-wheeled vehicle. The inner wheel operates on a tighter circle ($R - \frac{w}{2}$), where $w$ is track width. We use arctangent to find the sharper angle.

The outer wheel operates on a wider circle ($R + \frac{w}{2}$).
